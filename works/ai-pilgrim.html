AI Pilgrim

A sacred science-fiction world, carried from concept images to modular 3D assets, Blender repair and real-time scenes — to find out what AI can build, and where the artist has to take over.

01World

A civilisation of white machines and old stone.

AI Pilgrim imagines a sacred science-fiction culture: pilgrims in ceramic-white shells, ships that carry halos, ruins older than both.

It is also a test. I wanted to know how far AI tools could carry a world from first image to a real-time scene — and exactly where a person has to step in. The goal was never to treat generated models as finished assets, but to learn what they are good for: early development, modular iteration, visual exploration.

Style

Sacred science fiction

Dark sci-fi crossed with Gothic religious symbolism.

Palette

Cool white, charcoal, aged gold

Matte white as the base; black and gold only as accents.

Form

Ceremony and machinery

Halos, capes and relics rebuilt from joints, frames and damaged panels.

Atmosphere

Cold and quiet

Industrial spaces, restrained contrast, light used like architecture.

02Concepts

Images first, judged for what they can become.

I used image-generation workflows to explore characters, vehicles, drones and environments. Each image was read less as a picture and more as a brief: is the silhouette clear, can it be split into modules, will it survive 3D generation?

03Generation

Four candidates, one direction.

Several generated versions of the ship were compared for proportion, silhouette stability, mechanical detail, surface deformation and — most importantly — how well they could be taken apart.

04Breakdown

Take it apart to make it better.

Complex assets were divided into smaller components: main body, ring, wings, landing supports, joints, connectors. Smaller parts generate more accurately, are easier to inspect, and can be replaced one at a time. Characters and robots follow the same logic.

05Repair

Where the hand comes back in.

Parts that came out blurred, unstable or structurally wrong in a full-model generation were isolated and regenerated on their own, then reassembled in Blender — checked for scale, alignment, thickness, hierarchy and material slots before going anywhere near the engine.

  1. 01

    Review

    Inspect the full generation and mark the unstable regions.

  2. 02

    Isolate

    Separate the problem part from the whole.

  3. 03

    Refine

    Regenerate or replace it with a more readable version.

  4. 04

    Reassemble

    Put it back, and check the complete asset again.

06Stone

Structure before surface.

Environment assets took a different path. Large architectural forms were first generated as clean white models, to judge silhouette and proportion with nothing to hide behind. Only then did stone, moss, wear and roughness come in — adjusted in Blender and Unreal Engine, not accepted as a one-click output.

07Real time

Then walk around it.

Selected assets were brought into Unreal Engine 5 and judged where it counts: scale against a figure, silhouette under real lighting, how materials respond, whether the pieces hold together as a place.

08Findings

What generated assets still get wrong.

No synthetic score was given. Each output was reviewed against practical criteria, and the recurring problems were written down.

01

Thin geometry

Wings, panels and fine structures collapse or lose readability.

02

Detail deforms

More complexity brings unstable proportions and noisy surfaces.

03

Topology

Meshes need inspection, cleanup or replacement before assembly.

04

Part consistency

Separately generated parts disagree on scale and connection points.

05

Material translation

Roughness, reflection, transparency and layered wear do not transfer reliably.

06

One language

Keeping characters, vehicles and ruins in the same visual world is hard.

The most reliable workflow was not full automation. It was a controlled process in which generation, artistic judgement, modular design and real-time testing each did their part.

Boundary

An experimental workflow with documented limitations — not a claim of production-ready, fully automated asset creation.
